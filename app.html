Tumor Classification Types
The model classifies MRI scans into one of four categories
Glioma Tumor
Glial Cells · Brain Tissue
Originates from glial cells — the supportive tissue of the brain. One of the most common and aggressive brain cancers, often requiring multimodal treatment.
Aggressive Common Grade I–IV
Meningioma Tumor
Meninges · Brain Covering
Arises from the meninges — the protective layers surrounding the brain and spinal cord. Typically slow-growing and often benign, more common in adults over 60.
Slow-growing Often Benign Treatable
Pituitary Tumor
Pituitary Gland · Skull Base
Develops in the pituitary gland located at the base of the brain. Affects hormone production and regulation, often discovered due to vision disturbances or hormonal imbalance.
Usually Benign Hormonal Functional
No Tumor
Normal Brain · Healthy Tissue
MRI scan shows no abnormal growths or lesions. Normal brain structure and tissue density. Essential baseline class for training the model to distinguish healthy from pathological scans.
Healthy No Abnormality Baseline Class
